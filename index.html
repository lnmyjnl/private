Felidae

What is Felidae?

Felidae is the biological family of mammals commonly known as cats. It belongs to the order Carnivora and includes 41 living species, ranging from the small black-footed cat to the massive tiger.

Two Subfamilies

  • Pantherinae
    • Lions
    • Tigers
    • Leopards
    • Jaguars
    • Snow Leopards
  • Felinae
    • Domestic cats
    • Cheetahs
    • Cougars
    • Lynxes
    • Bobcats

What Actually Separates Them

The split between Pantherinae and Felinae comes down to a small but significant difference in anatomy: the structure of the larynx and a bone in the throat called the hyoid. In Pantherinae, this structure is flexible, which allows big cats like lions and tigers to roar — but it means they cannot purr continuously the way smaller cats do. In Felinae, the hyoid is fully ossified (hardened into bone), which allows continuous purring but makes roaring physically impossible. In short, a cat can typically do one or the other, not both.

A Common Misconception!

The fastest animal on land, the cheetah, actually belongs to the Felinae family, not Pantherinae. Though on first thought one might consider it a "big cat," the cheetah is classified with domestic cats, cougars, and lynxes — not with lions and tigers.
